How we verified this

Kenya Power — Auditor-General Reconciliation

A standalone record of sources, method, and what was and wasn't resolved, kept separate from the published piece itself.

Sources

  • Four consecutive years of the Auditor-General's statutory reports on Kenya Power (FY2022–FY2025)
  • Kenya Power's own Integrated Annual Reports for the same years, including the Chairman's Statement, CEO's Message, and Financial Highlights sections

No press coverage or third-party aggregation was used as a source for any figure.

Method

  • Primary-source preference — the Auditor-General's own reports and the company's own Integrated Reports only
  • Independent recomputation — every summed, subtracted, or derived figure rebuilt rather than trusted as stated
  • Direct narrative-absence verification — checked whether specific figures actually appear in the Chairman's Statement, CEO's Message, and Financial Highlights text, rather than assuming absence
  • Three separate audit passes, each re-verifying the prior pass — this process caught and corrected a mislabeled fiscal year, a conflated figure between two similar receivables totals, and a file-integrity error, all before publication

What was found

A Rural Electrification Scheme receivable balance grew every year checked: Kshs 26.9Bn (FY2023) → Kshs 30.7Bn (FY2024) → Kshs 34.5Bn (FY2025), labelled "Recurring" in the company's own internal audit tracking — with a Cabinet-approved Kshs 19.4Bn disbursement toward it still unpaid.

Across 10 sampled counties, Kenya Power's own books claimed Kshs 3.94Bn owed, against Kshs 1.57Bn the counties themselves confirmed — a Kshs 2.36Bn gap, of which Nairobi County alone accounted for roughly 95.5%.

None of these specific figures appear anywhere in the shareholder-facing narrative sections, though a general reference to "delayed payments" does.

What was disclosed as unresolved, not resolved

A Kshs 791 million gap between the FY2022 Auditor-General report's own figure and the FY2023 report's comparative citation of the same metric could not be explained by either document. This is stated as unresolved rather than assigned a preferred figure — the same disclosure standard applied to Kenya Power's own reporting was applied here.

Notice given before publication

Kenya Power's Board Office and Nairobi County's Finance office were both notified directly, in writing, with the specific findings concerning them, ten business days before this piece was published.
