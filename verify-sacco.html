How we verified this

SACCO Sector — NPL & Delinquency Comparison

A standalone record of sources, method, and what was and wasn't resolved, kept separate from the published piece itself.

Sources

  • Each SACCO's own published financial disclosures — Harambee, Stima, Mwalimu National, and Kenya Police DT
  • SASRA's own independently published supervision data for the same institutions and years

No press coverage or third-party aggregation was used as a source for any figure.

Method

  • Primary-source preference — each institution's own disclosures cross-checked directly against SASRA's own published figures
  • Independent recomputation — every ratio rebuilt from source figures rather than taken as reported
  • Two audit passes — the second pass caught two additional gaps the first draft missed
  • Right-of-reply briefings sent to all four SACCOs, and SASRA itself notified, before publication

What was found

Three of the four SACCOs' self-reported figures did not match SASRA's independently published numbers for the same institution and years.

What was disclosed as unresolved, not resolved

Kenya National Police DT SACCO's own delinquency ratio moved from 3.14% to 2.54% (falling), while SASRA's NPL figure for the same institution over the same years moved from 2.33% to 2.42% (rising) — opposite directions, same institution, same years. An explanation that resolves a similar-looking gap elsewhere in the piece was ruled out for this case specifically. No explanation is offered where none could be confirmed.

What looked like an error but wasn't

SASRA's own table lists Stima Sacco's 2024 total assets at KES 66.51bn; Stima's own report states KES 66.44bn. Neither figure is wrong — one is Group-level, the other Society-level. Checking these blind against each other would look like a discrepancy; it isn't.

A pattern across two institutions

Harambee's core capital ratio is 16.4% self-reported versus 18.34% from SASRA. Kenya Police DT's is 33% versus 34.36%. Both institutions clear the regulatory minimum either way — the gap itself, appearing twice on the same metric, is the finding worth naming rather than a compliance concern.
